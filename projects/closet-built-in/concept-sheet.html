Above the header · 10¾″ to ceiling

Everything above 80½″ is behind the header, so the only way a box comes down is through the gap between the top shelf and the front wall. That is why the top shelf is 12″ deep and not full depth: it leaves a 12″ gap. Bins about 11″ deep drop straight down through it; deeper ones need a tilt. A full-depth shelf would leave ¾″ and trap whatever is up there. See section B below.

Side sections

  • A: the hanging sections. Rods 12″ off the back wall, shelves 12″ deep so the front edge sits right over the rod. That leaves a 12″ gap under the header to slide a bin forward and drop it out.

Build phases

The closet is fully usable after phase 1. Pick a phase to draw it in the elevation.
Phase 1

Shell, rods and shelves

  1. On the bench: edge band the partition fronts, drill the shelf pin holes on their inner faces from 27″ to 68″, and notch the bottoms for the baseboard. The holes are far easier to drill flat than in place.
  2. Cleats and nailers into the studs. Level them off marks at 84″ and 70″ rather than measuring up from the floor.
  3. Stand the partitions. They are 84″ tall and the opening is 80½″, so bring each one in tilted and stand it up inside. The diagonal is 87¼″, which clears the 95½″ ceiling.
  4. Tie them together: the floor rail at the back and the fixed 70″ shelf, then the three top shelf pieces and the right 70″ shelf.
  5. Finish: glue on the nosings, hang the rods, and drop in the adjustable shelves at 28″, 42″ and 55¾″. The rolling hamper goes on the floor below them.
Phase 2

Hamper frame and 3 drawers

  1. Pull the two lower adjustable shelves. The third moves to 59⅝″; the spares can go on the right side's open floor.
  2. Build the hamper frame: the right side, the back 15½″ behind where the face goes, and the top rail on the left. The left side stays open.
  3. Build the three drawer boxes and mount the slides on the partitions, using a spacer board cut to each slide's height so both sides match. The hamper frame's slides go at the top of the frame.
  4. Fix the hardwood top at 49″ with pocket screws into the partitions from below.
  5. Hang the faces with ⅛″ gaps, starting with the hamper face ¼″ off the floor, then set the remaining shelf.

Cut list

Finished sizes in inches. Shelves are side to side × front to back, partitions are height × depth, faces are width × height. Anything that meets a wall is listed at its nominal size: measure in place and cut to fit.

Build notes

Carcass

  • 23/32″ plywood (sold as ¾″), prefinished maple or birch, with hardwood edge banding. All sizes here are for 23/32″.
  • Two partitions, floor to 84″, 23¼″ deep. That leaves ¾″ at the back to scribe to the wall. Notch them over the baseboard or pull the baseboard.
  • Top shelf 12″ deep, on cleats screwed to studs on all three walls and screwed down into the partition tops. Spans are 28″, 22⁹⁄₁₆″ and 28″.
  • Shelves over rods are 12″ deep too, with the rod right under the front edge. Keep the rod at 12″ rather than pulling it back under the shelf: coats are 20–22″ across the shoulders and need the room behind. Only the center column shelves at 70″ and below run full depth.
  • Glue a 1½″ hardwood nosing on the 28″ shelves so they don't sag under sweaters.

Center column

  • 22⁹⁄₁₆″ inside width gives 21⁹⁄₁₆″ drawer boxes with ½″ side-mount slides. Check your slides' spec before building boxes.
  • 21″ full-extension slides on a 23¼″ deep carcass. The hamper rolls on the floor, so its frame's slides carry only the frame.
  • Faces overlay ⅜″ of each partition edge and finish flush with the front wall's inside face.
  • No toe kick: the hamper face runs to ¼″ off the floor.

Check before you cut

  • Measure each return. If the opening is off-center, shift the center column to stay centered on the opening, not the closet.
  • Check the ceiling height at the front, back and both corners.
  • Check the walls for plumb and the back corners for square.
  • Find the studs on all three walls for the cleats and rod sockets.
  • Note any outlet, switch or attic hatch inside the closet.
